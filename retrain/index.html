04

Her Performance Review

“I know about everybody.”

Donna Paulsen, Suits
Goal

Every Friday she reads her own week against yours, proposes what to change about her playbook, and puts the review on your calendar for Monday. You approve. That is the loop, closed.

Station 4 of 4: Retrain. The one most people never build.

Why a Friday

A loop is open when work goes out and nothing comes back to tell you how it went. A loop is closed when the result comes back to the person who set the rules, and the rules get better.

Donna Paulsen would never sit through a performance review. So she gives herself one, every Friday, and you sign it. Your Sent folder is her report card.

The steps

  1. What she will look at

    Three sources, every Friday:

    • The triage reports she wrote this week.
    • The drafts she saved for you.
    • The emails you actually sent. Who you answered fast, what you spent your week on, how you really write.

    She compares what she did with what you did, and writes down only what the evidence shows: a person who should be on your Reply-Now list, a priority that changed, a draft that did not sound like you.

  2. Give her the Friday shift

    In the left sidebar: Scheduled → New task → Create with Claude. Paste the weekly order. The last line makes her do it right now, on this week so far:

    The weekly order 
    Every Friday at 3:00 PM my local time, use my donna-triage skill to retrain:
1. Read this week: the triage reports you wrote, the drafts you saved, and the emails I actually sent.
2. Compare. Who did I answer fast that is not on my Reply-Now list? Which threads did I spend my week on that are not in my priorities? Where did my sent emails sound different from your drafts?
3. Propose changes to my playbook: people, priorities, voice. Keep the hard rules exactly as they are: never send, never delete.
4. Write “Donna’s Week in Review” into a Google Calendar event, Monday 8:00 to 8:05 AM, proposed changes at the top, with an EMAIL reminder 1 minute before.
5. Save the proposed changes as a document called “Donna’s Week in Review” in a Google Drive folder called “Donna”.
Also do a dry run right now on this week so far, and for the dry run put
the event on today’s calendar 10 minutes from now instead of Monday.

    Yes, even on a Wednesday. Your Sent folder since Monday gives her plenty to say.

  3. Watch her first review land

    About ten minutes, three confirmations:

    • Donna’s Week in Review sits on today’s calendar.
    • Open the event: her proposed changes are at the top, numbered, each with a because.
    • One minute before it starts, the email arrives. A performance review, in your inbox, that you didn’t write.

    This is 1:13 PM ET (10:13 AM PT · 6:13 PM London · 7:13 PM Paris), minute 73 of 90. Four stations lit. Type ✓ in the Zoom chat.

  4. Monday: sign it

    Read the review in the email. If you agree, open your Donna project chat and paste:

    The signature 
    Approve this week’s review. It is the document “Donna’s Week in Review” in my Google Drive folder “Donna”. Write my updated skill file as a downloadable zip named donna-triage.zip.

    Download the zip, then Customize → Skills. Find donna-triage in the list, open its menu, and delete it. Then + → Create skill → Upload a skill with the new zip. One paste, one upload, once a week, only when she proposes a change.

    Approve by number: “Approve 1 and 3.” or “Reject 2.” work too. Nothing changes until you say so, and her hard rules never change at all.

What just happened

Connect, teach, run, retrain. For over fifty years, anything that learns from data has run some version of this: collect the data, train, put it to work, look at what it got wrong, retrain. (Textbooks add “evaluate” and “monitor”; we fold them into Run and Retrain.) You are not retraining Claude. You are retraining Donna, and Donna is a document you own.

You did it when…

A performance review you didn’t write is on your calendar, with the changes she wants to make at the top.

Why this mattersEveryone else is still telling AI what to do. You built the Friday where she tells you what she got wrong.
